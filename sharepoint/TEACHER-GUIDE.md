# Asking for a change to the course reader

A one-page guide for teachers. Paste this into a SharePoint page, or hand it
out as is.

---

## What this is

You can ask for changes to the course reader in plain language, in a chat
window. You do not need to edit a page, learn any software, or understand how
the site is built.

The chat is on the Biology page in SharePoint. Ask it the way you would ask a
colleague.

## Asking

Good requests share three things: **where**, **what is wrong**, and **what
would make it right**.

> "In 2.3, the paragraph about the stroma assumes students know what a
> concentration gradient is. Define it before using it."

That is enough. If you leave something out the assistant will ask — one
question at a time, not a form.

**One change per request.** If you want three things fixed, ask for one, and
ask for the next when the first is done. Requests that bundle several changes
are slow to review and hard to check.

## What happens next

1. The assistant repeats your request back in a sentence. Check it — this is
   the cheapest moment to correct it.
2. A draft is written. Usually 5–20 minutes. You can close the window; it
   works in the background.
3. You are shown **what changed**: the sentences before and after, not code.
4. You say **publish**, or you say what to change and get a second draft.

## Before you publish

Read the changed sentences as a biologist, not just as a reader. Shortening a
paragraph is the easiest way to break a mechanism without noticing, and
nothing automatic can catch that. Two questions:

- Does it still say what it said, in fewer words?
- Would a student who has not met this term before follow it?

## When it is wrong

Say so, in your own words, and it will be fixed:

> "The second one lost the point about the stomata. Put that back."

You can do this as many times as you like before publishing. Nothing is
published until you say so.

## What it cannot do

The assistant changes **the words in chapters**. It cannot:

- add or change diagrams, photos or interactive simulations
- change the layout, colours or reading tools
- add a new chapter or move one between units

Ask for those separately — they go to the person who looks after the site.

## Seeing what has changed

The course has a **Change log** page, linked in the reader under *Reference*.
It shows what has been asked for, what is being drafted, and what has already
gone live — so you can see what your colleagues are changing.

## If something looks wrong

Every published change can be undone. Say what went wrong in the chat, and the
person who looks after the site can put the page back the way it was.

---

*Nothing you ask for is published without you saying so.*
