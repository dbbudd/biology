Course goals

Biology is an introductory course that focuses on the principles and theories of biology, encourages investigation of biological relationships, and highlights the application of biology to everyday situations. Students in this course explore the biological concepts of biochemistry, cellular energetics, inheritance and variation of traits, interactions in ecosystems, and evolution.

The three dimensions

All science courses at HKIS use the NGSS (Next Generation Science Standards) framework, which is built from three dimensions. Every lesson and unit of study has all three present.

DCI

Disciplinary Core Ideas

The content — the actual science you learn in the course.

CCC

Crosscutting Concepts

The glue that binds ideas together, such as structure and function, or cause and effect. They are what let you make sense of the content rather than only recall it.

SEP

Science and Engineering Practices

What scientists actually do — investigating, or using evidence to make a scientific argument.

The goal by the end of the year is that you can do, know and think like a scientist. That is why the assessments ask you to analyse data and argue from evidence rather than only to recall definitions — the practices are assessed alongside the content.

You will see the crosscutting concept named at the top of many chapters — Patterns, Cause and Effect. It tells you what kind of thinking that section is asking for, so it is worth reading rather than skipping.

Assessment and reporting

This course uses standards-based grading. A variety of formative and summative assessments are used.

What it isHow it counts
Formative Tasks designed to give you feedback over time so you can set goals and move your learning forward — not to make a final judgement about your proficiency. Not part of the body of evidence for your proficiency level. May be used to override a report card grade where more evidence is needed, and only where it supports a higher proficiency.
Summative Tasks that capture your performance at one point in time after instruction, judged against a grade-level benchmark. Evaluated on the full General Academic Scale, and used to inform report card grades.

Reassessment

All High School students have an opportunity to reassess summative assessments. Reassessment decisions are linked to the type of learning goal:

  • Discrete learning goals require a discrete reassessment opportunity.
  • Spiralling learning goals — where content or skills are assessed again in a later unit — may be reassessed on that later assessment. A teacher may still give a reassessment at any time if they judge it necessary for your learning.

Reassessments may range from an individual student to an entire class. Normally only one formal reassessment opportunity is allowed per summative, though exceptions may be made within the teaching team.

To be eligible you must:

  • Have completed all agreed-upon formative learning before the summative.
  • Have completed the original task on the date it was scheduled, unless there were extenuating circumstances.
  • Complete relearning activities as determined by your teacher, and show evidence of improved performance.

A reassessed task may be reassessed partially, entirely, or in a different format, as the teacher determines. Teachers notify parents or guardians if a student is required to reassess. Reassessment should typically happen within one eight-day cycle of the assessment being returned; teaching teams may allow a second cycle in extenuating circumstances.

After reassessment, the new performance replaces the original grade in Schoology, and the original is recorded in the comments. If the reassessment grade is lower, the original grade stands and the reassessment score is noted in the comments.

Long-term projects where feedback has been given throughout, and final measures of learning such as end-of-course or semester exams and culminating projects, may not be reassessed.

Absences and missed assessments

Planned absence. If you will be away for more than three consecutive days, a planned absence form must be completed so there is a plan for while you are away.

Illness. If you miss an in-class assessment, follow up with your teacher when you return and make a plan to complete it. On the day of the absence, email your teacher if you are well enough.

Missed summatives. Students who miss three summatives will be asked to speak with the divisional leadership team, and parents will be contacted. Repeated patterns of missing assessment result in a behaviour contract. An unexcused absence on a scheduled summative day results in an IE for that assessment.

A pattern of late or missed assessments leads to administrative contact with parents and a conference with administration and your counsellor.

Technology and the use of AI

HKIS has clear guidelines for the use of artificial intelligence, and it is important that all students understand how AI can and cannot be used for learning. In summary, you are expected to use AI with integrity:

  • Use AI tools responsibly, and never in a way that could harm yourself or others.
  • Use AI only to support your learning, following school rules and your teacher's instructions on when and how it may be used for an assignment.
  • Be honest about when you have used AI, and never submit work fully created by AI as your own.
  • If you use AI, review its work for mistakes.
  • Check with your teacher when you are unsure what is acceptable.
Lab safety and working outside class

In the lab, act in a serious manner, participate in your group's work, and handle supplies and equipment with care — for safety and to avoid waste. On lab days, wear closed-toed shoes and appropriate clothing.

There will be times you need to work in the class or lab space outside of class hours:

  • To work in the desk area, you must have teacher permission.
  • To work in the lab area, you must have direct teacher supervision.
  • You may only work in the lab on projects directly related to this course. To pursue a project outside the course, ask your teacher about the application process.
Extra help

Seeking help is an important strategy for success in this course. Find your teacher during office hours or flex time. You are also encouraged to use the peer tutors scheduled in the Math and Science Tutoring Centre.

Academic integrity and the Secondary Assessment, Grading and Reporting Agreements are covered by school-wide HKIS policy.

How to use this reader

You do not have to read straight through. Each chapter stands on its own, and the sidebar stays with you wherever you are.

Use the Aa button in the toolbar to change text size, switch to a serif or dyslexia-friendly font, or pick a light, sepia or dark theme. Your choice is remembered on this device.

Reading tools

  • Search — the magnifier, or Ctrl/Cmd + K. Searches this page in full plus every other chapter's contents.
  • Progress — mark a lesson or activity complete with the pill in its heading. The bar in the toolbar tracks the whole course, split by unit; the sidebar tracks each unit. It is stored on this device only.
  • Link icons — the 🔗 beside any heading copies a direct link to that exact section, for pasting into Schoology.
  • Focus mode — hides the sidebar so the text has the screen to itself.
  • Underlined terms — hover or tap a term like natural selection to see its definition without losing your place.

Working with the videos and simulations

Videos load only when you click them, so a chapter opens quickly. Where a video should be watched in part, the timestamp is shown next to it. Simulations have controls that change a real variable in the model and a readout of what the model is doing — the questions underneath are worth attempting before you read the answer.

If a video is blocked on the school network, use the "Open on YouTube" link beneath it. If a simulation fails to load, the surrounding text and diagram still carry the full explanation — nothing essential lives only inside a simulation.
